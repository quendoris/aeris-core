# AERIS — Acknowledgements

AERIS treats attribution as historical bookkeeping, not decoration.

A polished modern presentation can make a recent vendor or website appear to be the origin of an idea. This file deliberately follows the intellectual lineage backward to the people whose work created the concepts AERIS depends on.

## Primary cartographic inspiration

### Allen K. Philbrick

Cartographic literature attributes the **Sinu-Mollweide projection** used as AERIS's present primary projection reference to **Allen K. Philbrick (1953)**.

Philbrick combined sinusoidal and Mollweide equal-area ideas in an arrangement distinct from Goode Homolosine, using normal and oblique aspects to reduce interruption and preserve useful continuity of major landmasses.

The University of Michigan's Deep Blue repository preserves a later reprint of Philbrick's *This Human World* and explicitly points readers to Philbrick's "sinumollweide" map projection as the basis for thematic maps in the book.

AERIS gives Philbrick prominent acknowledgement because this is an intellectual contribution to cartographic history, not because of present-day commercial visibility.

Historical references:

- Allen K. Philbrick, *This Human World*, reprint held by the University of Michigan Deep Blue repository: https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/3d64fa5f-588c-4d14-a843-153e92952a22
- J. P. Snyder's discussion of Mollweide-derived projections records Philbrick's 1953 combination of Sinusoidal and Mollweide in a manner distinct from Goode Homolosine.

## Projection lineage

### J. Paul Goode

**J. Paul Goode** introduced the **Goode Homolosine projection in 1923**, combining sinusoidal and Mollweide components at the latitude where their scales match. The projection became especially well known in interrupted form, with interruptions positioned to reduce distortion over major land areas.

Goode's work is an important direct predecessor in the history of combining sinusoidal and Mollweide equal-area constructions.

### Karl Brandan Mollweide

**Karl Brandan Mollweide** introduced the equal-area projection now bearing his name in **1805**. Its treatment of high latitudes became one of the mathematical ingredients later reused by Goode, Philbrick, and many other cartographers.

### Sinusoidal projection lineage

The sinusoidal equal-area projection predates the modern named constructions above and has a substantially older history. AERIS will not assign a single inventor where the historical record does not justify one.

## Modern visual reference

### The Future Mapping Company

The Future Mapping Company's **Wide-Angle World Wall Map** was the modern visual reference through which this project encountered the Sinu-Mollweide idea.

That is recorded here as a factual discovery path and visual reference. AERIS does not use their artwork, map data, layout assets, or proprietary product files, and no affiliation is implied.

Because this is a commercial product rather than the origin of the underlying cartographic idea, AERIS intentionally distinguishes this reference from the historical acknowledgement given to Philbrick and the projection lineage above.
